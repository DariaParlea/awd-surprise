Surprise has two faces.

For most people I asked, a surprise is yellow, pink, a gift with a bow. For me it is the moment I find out my hopes were too high. I explored both sides of the word.

What other people said

I asked people the same question: what comes to mind when you think about the word surprise? The answers were mixed. Everyone thought of something positive, but only some also thought about the negative side of the word.

The colours they chose

When people named a colour, it was always a bright one. These three became the palette of this site.

  • Yellow good news, light
  • Pink joy, a present
  • Purple the unknown
  • Light blue my own colour for it

What it means to me

When I hear the word surprise, I think of disappointment first.

I get excited. Then I start to get my hopes up. In the end I am either disappointed or I feel nothing at all, because the surprise did not happen, or because it was so predictable that I saw it coming and it lost its meaning.

  1. Excitement Something is coming.
  2. Hope I imagine what it will be, and the picture keeps growing.
  3. The ending It does not come, and I am disappointed. Or it comes, but I guessed it, and I feel nothing.

The waiting is what takes the surprise away. The more I expect, the less there is left to surprise me.

Where the word comes from

To be surprised is, quite literally, to be overtaken: caught before you could get ready.

In the dictionary

surprise noun
An event or piece of news that is unexpected or happens suddenly.
The feeling caused by something happening suddenly or unexpectedly.
surprise verb
To make someone feel surprised.
To attack, find or catch someone suddenly and unexpectedly.

How the meaning changed

  1. Latin prehendere, to grasp or seize.
  2. 1100s Old French sorprendre: sur (over) and prendre (to take).
  3. Late 1300s Reaches English as a military word: an unexpected attack or capture.
  4. 1590s Starts to mean anything unexpected.
  5. About 1600 Starts to mean the feeling of astonishment.
  6. 1708 Also the name of a dish that looked plain but was full of variety inside.
  7. 1857 The surprise party, which in 1826 had meant a hidden group of soldiers.

The same root is inside comprehend, apprehend and enterprise. Romanian took the word from French as surpriză.

Why a surprise fades when you expect it

My feeling about surprises turns out to have a name in three different sciences.

In the brain

Dopamine neurons fire when a reward is better than expected. A reward you fully expected gets no response at all. An expected reward that never comes makes their activity drop below normal.

That drop is my disappointment.

In emotion research

Psychologist Paul Ekman counts surprise among the basic emotions. It is the shortest one, lasting a few seconds before joy or fear takes over. You cannot feel it about something you correctly predicted.

That is my "feel nothing".

In mathematics

Information theory measures surprise as a number called surprisal. The less likely an event, the higher it is. An event you are certain of has a surprisal of zero.

A predictable surprise is, mathematically, no surprise.

From a birthday to the impossible

I started with the most ordinary surprise and kept moving it further away from home.

  1. Specific

    A birthday surprise, or a gift you have been waiting for. This is the one in my own notes.

  2. Wider

    In war, where the word began: armies still talk about the element of surprise. In stories, the plot twist. In music, a chord you did not see coming.

  3. Unlikely

    Surprise is the name of a real city in Arizona. Language models are trained by measuring how surprised they are by the next word. In 1708, a surprise was something you could eat.

  4. Impossible

    Surprising yourself on purpose. A surprise party everyone knows about, including you. Being surprised by the same thing twice.

Both faces are true

The people I asked remember the bow and the yellow. I remember the waiting. The science says we are describing the same thing from two ends: a surprise is bright only while you cannot predict it.

So the best surprise might be the one nobody tells you is coming. Including this page, which you probably did not expect to end with a question: when was the last time something truly surprised you?
